Mastery & Legacy Project

From manual scheduling to a same-day system

Problem

At an IRC education program, student assessment data — spanning pre-screener, scheduling, pre-assessment, and post-assessment — was spread across multiple sources and highly manual, making it difficult to verify accuracy or trace a single student's full trajectory through the program. Without trustworthy, connected data, the program couldn't reliably act on it in time to help students who needed support.

Role & Actions

As an M&E team intern, I built a master data sheet that became the single source of truth linking the full pre-screener-to-post-assessment cycle, replacing a fragmented, manual, hard-to-verify structure. On top of it, I integrated two additional signals — students' self-assessed school readiness and teachers' classroom observation scores — and used pivot tables and Apps Script to automate analysis, so data could be reviewed as it arrived rather than only at the end of a full cycle.

Impact

— Data quality improved substantially — the master structure made errors easy to catch rather than easy to miss
— By comparing gaps between student self-assessment, teacher observation, and objective pre-assessment scores, the team could identify students whose self-perception and actual performance diverged early enough to intervene — through a schedule adjustment or other targeted support — rather than waiting until post-assessment to notice
— Built a reusable data pipeline with potential to scale to other program sites — the direct basis for one of my 1-year career experiments below

Q · Drucker — Feedback Analysis

"What is your greatest strength?"

I expected that consolidating student data — spread across pre-screener, scheduling, pre-assessment, and post-assessment — into one accurate source would mainly be a data-cleaning exercise. In practice, once that master data sheet existed, it enabled something I hadn't anticipated: comparing students' self-assessed school readiness against teacher observations and objective pre-assessment scores, and using the gaps between them to catch students whose self-perception and actual performance diverged early enough to intervene.

Strength: I don't just centralize data — I look for what becomes possible once it's trustworthy and connected. This reflects how I perform best — as a listener rather than a reader, and as an adviser who synthesizes multiple signals rather than relying on a single measure.

Q · DiSC Flex

"Describe a time you dealt with conflict."

My natural DiSC profile is S-dominant (Steadiness) — I default to waiting for direction rather than initiating. During a weekly check-in cycle with my supervisor at IRC, I recognized my default style wasn't serving the project's pace, and consciously flexed toward D (Dominance): instead of waiting for her to set the week's agenda, I proactively proposed a plan — building out a first version of the master data sheet linking pre-screener through post-assessment data, then bringing concrete results to our next check-in for review. This let me take ownership of the data pipeline while still respecting her time.

Q · VUCA · GRIT

"How do you handle a fast-paced environment?"

I've operated in genuinely VUCA conditions across my career — from the compressed timelines of strategy consulting at KPMG and Accenture, to leading a Post-Merger Integration program at GAP under a hard 6-month deadline, to now navigating the ambiguity of a new sector at IRC. What's kept me resilient isn't a fixed skillset — it's GRIT: staying loyal to a long-term direction while letting the specific role, sector, or approach change. The clearest test of this was my pivot from consulting into a corporate role — the hardest part wasn't learning new skills, it was unlearning old ones. The fast, decisive "consulting mode" was actually a liability in an environment built on long-term relationships and internal alignment. Growth meant showing up differently every day until the new mode became second nature.

Q · Christensen — Personal Integrity

"How do you make difficult decisions?"

Situation

At GAP Greater China (post-acquisition by Baozun), I led the IT PMO workstream of a Post-Merger Integration program, migrating 15+ core business systems from US to local platforms within 6 months. The final three months of testing and debugging overlapped directly with Q4 preparation for major online events like Double 11, leaving almost no margin for error.

Task

The real challenge wasn't managing two workstreams at once — it was choosing to be transparent about the real risk of instability during the cutover window, rather than downplaying it to keep stakeholders comfortable.

Action

I disclosed the stability risks directly to business stakeholders ahead of time, rather than explaining them after an incident during peak sales. I coordinated with IT and the vendor to increase headcount and extend support hours, so testing and Q4 readiness could proceed in parallel rather than compete for resources. I translated technical risk into language business teams could act on, so both sides prepared together.

Result

Q4 events ran smoothly with the new system stable through the highest-pressure window. The program was completed within 6 months, generating approximately USD 400K in annualized cost savings, and established a pattern of proactive risk disclosure in how business and IT collaborated afterward.
